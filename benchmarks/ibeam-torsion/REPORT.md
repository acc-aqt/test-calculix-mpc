## How the values are measured

- **Twist** φ(x) = (u_z,top − u_z,bottom)/h_m, from the mean u_z of the flange mid-plane centre nodes (the nodes on each flange mid-plane over the web footprint, |z| ≤ t_w/2) at each x station, read from the `.frd`. The tip twist is φ(L). A torque about +x moves the top flange towards +z, so φ is positive.
- **Warping stress** σ_w at each flange-tip edge (z = ±b/2) at x = L/4: the mean nodal (extrapolated) σ_xx from the `.frd` over the edge's nodes through the flange thickness, which matches the mid-plane sectorial coordinate of thin-walled theory.
- **Sign pattern.** σ_w = −E·y·z·φ''(x), and φ'' > 0 for T about +x. The top flange bends laterally towards +z, which compresses its +z edge. So, in the table's order, the top flange tip at z = −b/2 is in tension (+), the top flange tip at z = +b/2 in compression (−), the bottom flange tip at z = −b/2 in compression (−) and the bottom flange tip at z = +b/2 in tension (+): **+ − − +**.
- **St-Venant shear** τ_SV (information) is τ_xz on the flange outer surfaces at x = L/2, at the centres of the flange outstands (z = ±(b/2 + t_w/2)/2, away from the web): the mean on the top surface minus the mean on the bottom surface, halved, since the St-Venant shear is +G·t_f·φ' on the top outer surface and −G·t_f·φ' on the bottom one. Warping shear from the flanges' lateral bending is mixed in, but at midspan it is small next to τ_SV. Most of the fine-level gap follows the FE twist rate at midspan, which is above the Vlasov φ' for the J reason given below. The coarse level's larger gap comes from its single element through the flange thickness, which resolves the through-thickness shear poorly.
- **Root peak von Mises** (information) is the largest nodal von Mises stress on the root face. The fixed face is singular where it meets the free surfaces, so it grows as the mesh is refined. Thin-walled theory gives σ_w = 148.84 MPa at the flange tips there.
- **No reference-node measurement.** ccx reports the `*COUPLING` reference node's displacement as zero, so the twist is measured on the beam itself.
- **Zero-twist guard.** A run whose |φ(L)| is below 1% of φ_SV(L) stops with an error and writes no report, because the torque did not reach the beam.
- **Load introduction.** The torque goes in through `*COUPLING` + `*DISTRIBUTING` on a face-based tip surface, not `*DISTRIBUTING COUPLING`, which drops a moment without a warning in ccx 2.24. See [ADR 0003](../../docs/adr/0003-torsion-coupling.md).

## Expected gap to thin-walled theory

The targets come from thin-walled formulas, applied here to a solid section, so a few percent of difference is expected even on a converged mesh. The sources:

- **No end correction in Σbt³/3.** The thin-walled J ignores the plate ends, where the shear flow turns round. With the end correction (1 − 0.63·t/b) per plate, J is 4.6 % lower. The solid is softer in St-Venant torsion than the target assumes, so it twists more, and with the smaller λ warping carries more of the torque, which raises σ_w. With the end-corrected J, Vlasov gives φ(L) = 0.27313 rad and σ_w(L/4) = 61.37 MPa (information). This is the main cause of the gap.
- **The web-flange junction** adds material and torsional stiffness that the formula ignores. It works the other way and partly offsets the end correction.
- **No warping shear deformation.** Thin-walled theory takes the flange mid-planes as shear-rigid in warping; the solid deforms in shear as the flanges bend laterally, which softens it a little more.
- **The tip load-introduction zone.** The coupling spreads the torque over the tip face differently from the St-Venant shear flow, and the disturbance is not short. In the committed run the flange-tip σ_w stays above Vlasov from about x ≈ 1200 mm to the tip (on the fine level more than 2 % above the end-corrected curve), levelling off at about 12–17 MPa between x = 2000 and 2800 mm, where Vlasov falls to a few MPa, and dropping to near zero only at the tip face. All three mesh levels agree, so it is not a discretisation error. x = L/4 is clear of it: there the fine level is -0.47 % from the end-corrected curve. The φ(x) gap also grows past the end-corrected line over the last third of the length. The likely cause, not proven, is that the coupling brings most of the torque into the flanges as lateral shear, where Vlasov carries it almost entirely as St-Venant shear at a free tip; in an open section that difference settles over about 1/λ ≈ 809 mm rather than locally. See figures 1 and 3 of [FIGURES.md](FIGURES.md) and the follow-up [#29](https://github.com/acc-aqt/test-calculix-mpc/issues/29).

Because the coarse mesh is slightly stiffer in torsion, the errors against the thin-walled targets grow a little as the mesh is refined, rather than shrink: the FE converges towards the solid section's answer, which lies near the end-corrected values, not towards the thin-walled targets.
